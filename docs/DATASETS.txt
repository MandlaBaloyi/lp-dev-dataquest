THE DATASETS — WHERE THEY COME FROM AND HOW THEY JOIN
The Missing List · DataQuest · Theme: Education
=========================================================

Every source is published openly by the South African government. None
required a login, a licence, or an access agreement. All of it is re-fetched
by script, so nothing here depends on a file somebody once downloaded.


1. WHAT WE STARTED WITH
---------------------------------------------------------

SOURCE A — EMIS SCHOOL MASTERLISTS
  Publisher   Department of Basic Education, EMIS unit
  Where       education.gov.za/Programmes/EMIS/EMISDownloads.aspx
  Form        234 Excel files: one per province, per quarter
  Covers      2007-2025, all nine provinces
  Holds       one row per school per year - enrolment, staffing, funding
              band, phase, district, coordinates, open/closed status
  Fetched by  src/fetch/emis_masterlists.py

SOURCE B — NSC SCHOOL PERFORMANCE REPORTS
  Publisher   Department of Basic Education
  Where       education.gov.za/Resources/Reports.aspx
  Form        13 PDF reports, 2012-2024
  Covers      2008-2024 (each edition carries three to five years)
  Holds       one row per school per year - matric candidates, learners
              progressed, learners achieving, pass rate
  Fetched by  src/fetch/nsc_reports.py
  Extracted by src/build/nsc_results.py

  This one is not published as a table. It is printed inside PDFs, and no
  two editions share a column layout. Extracting it is a project in itself,
  and is documented in section 5.

SOURCE C — STATS SA GENERAL HOUSEHOLD SURVEY
  Used for context on why learners leave, at province level. Not a model
  input: it has no school identifier, so it cannot be joined to a school.


2. WHAT THEY BECAME
---------------------------------------------------------

  datasets/processed/school_year_panel.csv.gz
    478,761 rows x 20 columns
    Every school of every type, 2007-2025, as published. 29,655 schools.
    This is the raw joined panel - deliberately uncleaned, so the audit has
    something honest to run against.

  datasets/processed/nsc_results.csv
    93,846 rows x 12 columns
    Matric results, 8,093 schools, 17 years.

  datasets/processed/clean_panel.csv.gz
    The panel after cleaning, restricted to schools that can hold a matric
    class. Cleaning rules and their row costs are in CLEANING_DECISIONS.md.

  datasets/processed/model_frame.csv.gz
    146,944 rows x 47 columns
    The clean panel plus every engineered feature and the target. This is
    what the model trains on.


3. HOW THEY JOIN
---------------------------------------------------------

THE KEY IS THE EMIS NUMBER
  Every school in South Africa has one. It is the only identifier shared
  across both sources, and everything depends on it.

IT IS NOT PUBLISHED CONSISTENTLY
  The same school appears as 0100000217 in one file and 100000217 in
  another. Joining on the raw string matches a fraction of what it should,
  and - this is the dangerous part - it fails silently. You get a smaller
  table, not an error.

  So every identifier is normalised before any join: strip non-digits,
  strip leading zeros. src/common/emis.py, and it is unit tested.

THE JOIN, STEP BY STEP
  1. Fold 234 provincial spreadsheets into one panel, keyed on
     (emis, year). Later quarters supersede earlier ones for the same year.
  2. Extract matric results from 13 PDFs into (emis, year) rows.
  3. Normalise both identifiers.
  4. Left join matric onto the panel.
  5. Carry each school's most recent matric record forward, recording how
     old it is (see section 4).

MATCH RATES, MEASURED
  ArcGIS reference list -> panel          1,480 / 1,480   100%
  No-fee schools list   -> panel            893 / 894      99%
  NSC results           -> panel                          ~92%

  The 8% that do not match are mostly exam centres and closed schools that
  appear in results but not in a current masterlist.


4. THE TIMING RULE
---------------------------------------------------------

The outcome is measured from year Y to Y+2. So every feature must be built
from year Y or earlier, or the model is predicting the past from the future.

That rule decides two things that look arbitrary otherwise:

  Every change is a backward difference. A three-year enrolment trend at
  2020 uses 2017 to 2020, never 2021.

  Matric records are carried forward, never backward. Scoring 2025 uses a
  school's 2024 matric figures, because the 2025 exams have not happened.
  A 'matric_age' column records how stale the carried figure is, so the
  model can discount an old one rather than treat it as current.

  Without the carry-forward the 2025 register - the actual product - would
  score every school with its matric evidence missing. With it, 79% of the
  2025 rows carry matric data at a median age of one year.


5. THE MATRIC EXTRACTION, AND THE BUG IN IT
---------------------------------------------------------

The reports do not share a layout. Across 13 editions we found four:

  1 measure per year    wrote
  2 measures per year    progressed, wrote
  3 measures per year    wrote, achieved, pass rate
  4 measures per year    progressed, wrote, achieved, pass rate

Worse, a single file can contain more than one. The 2012 edition declares
five years of "Wrote" in its header and then prints data pages carrying
three years of wrote, achieved and pass rate.

OUR FIRST PARSER TRUSTED THE HEADER, AND WAS WRONG
  Forcing one layout onto rows that used another shifted every field. The
  result passed every row count:

    14,005 records claimed more learners were advanced into matric than
           sat the exam - impossible
    87,055 records carried a quintile outside 1-5
         0 records had a pass rate, which looked so much like a field the
           source does not publish that it was removed from the model

THE FIX, AND THE PROOF
  Each row's layout is now derived from its own shape, preferring the widest
  published form. Two guards catch what shape-fitting cannot:

    progressed may never exceed wrote
    where a report prints achieved and pass rate, the arithmetic must
    agree - the rate is achieved divided by wrote

  That second check is the one that settles it. A misaligned row cannot
  satisfy it by chance. It now holds on 82,979 of 82,979 checkable records.

    records            71,291 -> 93,846
    progressed > wrote 14,005 -> 0
    quintile valid          - -> 96.0%
    achieved             7.6% -> 88.4%
    pass rate            0.0% -> 88.4%

  Pass rate turned out to be the strongest single variable in the model.
  It had been discarded on evidence that was our own bug.


6. WHAT IS NOT COMMITTED
---------------------------------------------------------

datasets/raw/ is gitignored. It runs to several hundred megabytes and every
byte is re-downloadable without a login, so the fetch scripts are version
controlled in its place. Two commands rebuild it exactly:

    python -m src.fetch.emis_masterlists
    python -m src.fetch.nsc_reports

The built tables in datasets/processed/ ARE committed, because those are the
project's own work rather than somebody else's file.
