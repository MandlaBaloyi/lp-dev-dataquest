"Business Model" — what the judges are actually asking for
=========================================================

In everyday language a business model answers one question: how does this
survive in the real world? Not "what is the cool idea", but who does it help,
who pays, how does it keep running, and why would it still exist after the
hackathon ends.

The judging criteria weight it at 5 marks out of 50. It is the smallest
category and the easiest to score nothing in, because a team that has built
something real usually has nothing to say about money.


THE SEVEN THINGS TO COVER
---------------------------------------------------------
  the problem          stated briefly
  the solution         what is being offered
  who it is for        the user, and separately the beneficiary
  value over existing  why this beats what is already there
  how it is funded     grant, adoption, subscription, or absorbed
  how it scales        one province to national
  team and resources   data access, partnerships, tech


FILLED IN, WITH OUR ACTUAL NUMBERS
---------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM
  More than 40% of South African learners who start Grade 1 never finish
  matric. The figure is published and undisputed. What does not exist is the
  same figure school by school, so support cannot be aimed.

SOLUTION
  A ranked risk register for every secondary school in the country. Each
  school gets a score, a national rank, and the three factors driving it.

  THE MODEL IS A WEIGHT-OF-EVIDENCE LOGISTIC SCORECARD.
  Not a random forest. A forest was trained and scores better - 0.7161
  against 0.6939 - and is not what ships, because it cannot tell a school
  why it was flagged. The gap is the measured price of interpretability and
  is reported rather than hidden.

  IT SCORES SCHOOLS, NEVER LEARNERS.
  An earlier draft of this document said the model "scores individual
  learners". It does not, and it never will. Learner records are protected
  under POPIA, a hackathon team has no lawful basis to process them, and a
  register of named at-risk children is a liability the moment it exists.
  The smallest unit is a school.

WHO IT IS FOR
  User        provincial planners and district officials who decide where
              teachers, funding and support staff go
  User        organisations running retention programmes, choosing which
              schools to place them in
  Beneficiary the learners who get help before they leave, rather than being
              counted afterwards

VALUE OVER WHAT EXISTS
  What exists is a spreadsheet with a red-amber-green rule, filled in by hand,
  covering the schools somebody already chose to look at. Ours covers all
  7,681 secondary schools, scores them against each other, and explains each
  score. The comparison across every school is the part nobody currently has.

  Measured: the worst-scored tenth loses learners at 26.8% against 1.95% in
  the best-scored tenth. Working the top of the list is roughly twice as
  productive as working at random.

HOW IT IS FUNDED
  Not sold to schools or families. It is a tool for the people who allocate
  support, not a product for the people who need it.

  Year 1   grant funded, R500,000-750,000, two-province pilot
  Year 2   four provinces at R150,000 each, plus NGO tier at R25,000
  Year 3   nine provinces, national rollout

  Running cost R612,000 a year, which is R49 per school scored. Data
  acquisition is genuinely zero - every source is public and fetched by
  script. Break-even at four provinces.

HOW IT SCALES
  It is already national. All nine provinces are in the data from day one,
  because the source publishes all nine. What scales is depth, not width:
  per-grade risk once that dataset is granted, annual retraining as new
  masterlists appear, and eventually a feed from the national learner
  tracking systems rather than files downloaded quarterly.

  The tenth customer costs almost nothing to serve, because adding a province
  is a filter rather than a project.

TEAM AND RESOURCES
  Data        all public, already collected, rebuilt by script in minutes
  Skills      data engineering, statistics, and someone who can talk to a
              provincial department
  Tech        Python, scikit-learn, Streamlit. No paid infrastructure
  Partnership an organisation already working on dropout would bring real
              outcome data to validate against
