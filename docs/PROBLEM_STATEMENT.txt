EARLY WARNING SYSTEM FOR SCHOOL DROPOUT — SOUTH AFRICA
Hackathon submission: problem, solution, business model
Theme: Education
=========================================================


1. THE PROBLEM
---------------------------------------------------------
South Africa loses about 40 of every 100 learners between
Grade 1 and matric.
(Source: DG Murray Trust, Zero Dropout Campaign)

We know the number. We do not know which schools.

That is the gap. Help is sent to schools that ask for it, or
that someone already knows about. It is not sent to the schools
losing the most learners, because nobody has that list.

You cannot send a teacher to a percentage.

Note: this is not about matric pass rates. We are not arguing
with anyone's exam results. Pass rate measures how learners did
in the exam. We are asking a different question: who never got
to write it.


2. OUR SOLUTION
---------------------------------------------------------
We turn that number into a list.

Using 19 years of open government data covering every school in
all nine provinces, we score each secondary school on how likely
it is to lose learners before matric. We rank the schools worst
first, and we show why each one scored the way it did.

A province can then start with the 50 schools that need help
most, instead of guessing.

What the model says:
  "This school is in the worst 10% nationally for losing
   learners before matric. The main reasons are its funding
   level, its learner-to-teacher ratio, and a falling matric
   class over the last three years."

What it does not say:
  It does not name individual learners. Learner records are
  private and protected by law (POPIA), and we have no access to
  them. It also does not yet break risk down by grade — Grade 10
  against Grade 11 — because per-grade enrolment sits in a
  dataset that requires separate permission. Both are honest
  limits, and both are on the roadmap.


3. WHY THIS APPROACH
---------------------------------------------------------
- We work at school level because school data is public and
  learner data is not. This is a legal limit, not a shortcut.
- The output is a ranking, because ranking is what lets someone
  act. A list of 50 schools is a decision. A national percentage
  is not.
- We show the reasons behind each score. A school ranked high
  for a funding reason needs different help from one ranked high
  for a staffing reason. Without the reason, the ranking tells
  you where to go but not what to bring.
- The need is already recognised. The Zero Dropout Campaign has
  told the Department of Basic Education that an early warning
  system is needed. Some NGOs already run one by hand, in
  spreadsheets, school by school. We are building the layer
  above that: the one that tells a province which schools to
  look at in the first place.


4. THE DATA
---------------------------------------------------------
All of it is public. None of it needed a login.

WHERE IT CAME FROM
  The Department of Basic Education publishes a schools
  masterlist for every province, every quarter. We found 234 of
  those files. Each holds one row per school with that year's
  learner count and educator count.

  We also pulled all 13 NSC School Performance Reports from 2012
  to 2024. Each report carries three years of results per school,
  and they chain without gaps, so five of them cover 2010 to 2024
  completely.

HOW WE JOINED IT
  Every school has an EMIS number. That is the key. We
  normalised it (the leading zero is inconsistent between
  sources), folded all 234 files together, and got:

    478,761 school-year rows
    19 consecutive years, 2007 to 2025
    all nine provinces
    29,655 schools

  Of those, 12,433 are secondary or combined schools — the ones
  that actually have a matric class, and the only ones this
  model is about.

WHAT WE HAD TO FIX
  Four faults in the source data. None of them caused an error.
  Each one would have quietly changed what the model learned.

  - The 2019 files spell the key column NatEMIS. Every other
    year spells it NatEmis. Matching exactly dropped the whole
    year, and the data still looked fine at 450,000 rows.
  - Province codes are not stable. KwaZulu-Natal appears as both
    KZ and KZN, Gauteng as both GT and GP. The result was eleven
    provinces in a country that has nine.
  - Gauteng 2023 has its key column headed "G" — cut off at the
    source. Three thousand schools disappeared. We recovered them
    by checking what was in the column instead of trusting its
    name.
  - School phase is called Phase_PED in newer files and Phase_DoE
    in older ones. Phase is how a secondary school is identified,
    so losing it loses the entire group this project is about.

  There is also a lot of everyday mess: school phase appears in
  22 different spellings, quintile in 18, the no-fee flag in 12.
  881 schools are recorded at latitude 0, which is a point in the
  Atlantic Ocean. All of this is cleaned in the open, and every
  cleaning decision is recorded.


5. BUSINESS MODEL
---------------------------------------------------------

5.1 WHO IT IS FOR
  USERS — the people who would run it:
    - Provincial education departments, deciding where to send
      teachers, funding and support staff
    - The Department of Basic Education, tracking dropout as a
      measured number rather than an estimate
    - NGOs already working on dropout, who currently do this by
      hand in spreadsheets, one school at a time
    - District officials and principals, who get a ranked list
      instead of an impression

  BENEFICIARIES — who it is ultimately for:
    - The learners who get help before they leave, rather than
      being counted after

5.2 WHY IT IS BETTER THAN WHAT EXISTS
  What exists now is a spreadsheet with a red/amber/green rule,
  filled in by hand, in whichever schools an NGO happens to work.
  It covers the schools someone already chose to look at.

  Ours covers every secondary school in the country, scores them
  against each other, and explains each score. That comparison
  across all schools is the thing nobody currently has.

5.3 HOW IT IS PAID FOR
  Not by learners, families or schools. This is a tool for the
  people who allocate support, not a product sold to the people
  who need it.

  It fits into funding that already exists. The DG Murray Trust
  already funds work on this exact problem. Provincial education
  departments already have data budgets. The realistic routes
  are a grant from an education funder, adoption by a provincial
  department as a planning tool, or an NGO taking it into a
  programme they already run.

  It does not need a subscription to survive. It needs to be
  useful enough that a funded organisation wants to keep it
  running.

5.4 HOW IT SCALES
  It is already national. All nine provinces are in the data
  from the first day, because the source publishes all nine.

  What scales from here is depth, not width:
    - Add per-grade risk once SNAP access is granted
    - Re-train each year as new data is published — the pipeline
      re-runs end to end with two commands
    - Connect to SA-SAMS and LURITS, the national learner
      tracking systems, so it runs on live data rather than
      files downloaded once a quarter

5.5 WHAT IT TAKES TO RUN
  DATA: all public, already collected, rebuilt by a script in
  minutes.

  SKILLS: data engineering for the pipeline, statistics for the
  model, and someone who can talk to a provincial department.

  TOOLS: Python, all free. No paid infrastructure.

  PARTNERSHIP WORTH HAVING: the Zero Dropout Campaign and DG
  Murray Trust. They have real outcome data and credibility with
  DBE, and could check our results against what they see on the
  ground.


6. WHAT WE ARE HONEST ABOUT
---------------------------------------------------------
- We predict for schools, not learners. Learner data is private.
- We do not yet split risk by grade. That needs a dataset that
  requires separate permission, which we have not been granted.
- Our measure of dropout is built from enrolment and matric
  numbers, not from following individual learners through school.
  It is a good indicator. It is not a census.
- The model will be wrong sometimes. If it flags a school that
  turns out fine, the cost is support that was not strictly
  needed. If it misses a school that is losing learners, that is
  the real failure, and it is why the model has to be retrained
  and checked every year rather than built once.
- "Grade 10 to 12 is the worst stretch" comes from published
  research, not from our own data. Our model is how we would
  check that claim against real numbers.
